# Structural Reference Analysis

The three screenshots in `references/` are design research, not production assets.

## What to learn from Wave

- A direct category headline and short promise sit beside a product UI mockup.
- The page alternates between compact benefit blocks, human proof, product workflows, and full-width color bands.
- Product value is grouped by buyer outcome: create professional work, manage receivables, get paid, and accept payments.
- Testimonials interrupt feature density and reset attention.
- FAQ and a strong bottom CTA close the page after visitors have seen the product repeatedly.

## What to learn from FreshBooks

- The hero names both the software category and audience.
- A small benefit grid immediately explains why the category matters for that audience.
- A second feature system combines short copy, screenshots, and a highlighted card.
- Long pages use large changes in background color to mark chapters.
- Proof, integrations, support, industry navigation, resources, FAQs, and a final CTA each have their own job.
- The artists and small-business pages share a system while varying the audience promise and supporting examples.

## Receipt Cycle interpretation

Use a shared component system, then vary composition and proof by intent. The result should feel related across all 11 pages without turning into keyword-swapped clones.

Recommended reusable modules:

- `LandingHeader`
- `IntentHero`
- `ProductProofFrame`
- `OutcomeCardGrid`
- `WorkflowSteps`
- `FeatureSplit`
- `UseCaseCards`
- `ConnectedWorkflow`
- `LandingFaq`
- `LandingFinalCta`
- `LandingFooter`

Recommended rhythm:

1. Light hero with real product proof.
2. Compact outcome band.
3. Alternating product sections.
4. Distinctive deep-teal chapter for workflow or proof.
5. Audience or use-case section.
6. Connected workflow and related pages.
7. FAQ.
8. High-contrast final CTA and shared footer.

## Required FreshBooks-style page rhythm

For the initial 10-page build, FreshBooks is the primary macro-layout reference. Each page should normally map the reference structure to an accurate Receipt Cycle equivalent:

1. Slim utility or announcement bar.
2. Restrained two-column hero with the commercial category, audience, CTA, and product proof.
3. Large colored benefit-card grid with enough copy to explain the buying value.
4. Centered feature heading followed by an asymmetric product-feature mosaic.
5. Full-width high-contrast CTA band.
6. Detailed product explanation or comparison section with generous white space.
7. Proof section. Use verified product evidence when customer reviews are unavailable.
8. Support or platform section.
9. Audience or industry accordion when the needs are meaningfully different.
10. Relevant resource cards.
11. Plainly titled Frequently Asked Questions section.
12. Final CTA band and full footer.

Match roughly 60 to 70 percent of this macro rhythm while keeping Receipt Cycle colors, spacing tokens, interface language, copy, and assets original. Do not copy exact FreshBooks dimensions, graphics, content, or distinctive compositions.

## What must stay original

- Receipt Cycle teal and emerald palette, typography, logo, radii, icons, and dashboard language.
- Original copy and metadata.
- Accurate Receipt Cycle screenshots or purpose-built mockups based on the real interface.
- Original illustration language if an illustration is genuinely needed.
- Page-specific product proof, ordering, examples, and CTA language.

## Responsive interpretation

- Keep the primary CTA and category promise visible before the first mobile scroll where practical.
- Crop or recompose product visuals for mobile rather than shrinking desktop screenshots into illegibility.
- Stack outcome cards in a purposeful reading order.
- Keep interactive targets at least 44 pixels high.
- Preserve section contrast and conversion rhythm without copying the reference layouts.
