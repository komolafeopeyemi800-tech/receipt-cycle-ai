# Product Marketing Context

*Last updated: 2026-09-28*

## Product Overview

**One-liner:** Receipt Cycle is a web and mobile finance workspace that connects receipt capture, expense tracking, invoicing, payments, budgets, reporting, and AI-assisted analysis.

**What it does:** Users can capture and organize expenses, manage customers and catalog items, create invoices and estimates, record payments, generate receipts, monitor budgets, review reports, and ask questions grounded in their saved financial records. The product connects the expense cycle and sales cycle so records remain useful after initial capture.

**Product category:** Receipt scanner, business expense tracker, invoice software, and small-business finance software.

**Product type:** SaaS with web and mobile applications.

**Business model:** Free and paid plans. Treat prices, limits, trials, and platform availability as implementation details that must be verified in the current code before publishing copy.

## Target Audience

**Target companies:** Freelancers, self-employed workers, consultants, service businesses, and small businesses that need organized expense and sales records without a heavyweight accounting suite.

**Decision-makers:** Business owner, freelancer, operations lead, or finance administrator. In very small teams, the buyer and daily user are usually the same person.

**Primary use case:** Keep evidence, expenses, invoices, payments, budgets, and reports connected in one practical workflow.

**Jobs to be done:**

- Capture receipts and transactions before details are lost.
- Create sales documents and see what is paid, outstanding, or overdue.
- Prepare clean records and reports for review, reconciliation, or an accountant.
- Understand spending patterns and budget progress without manually rebuilding the story.

**Use cases:** Receipt OCR, business expense tracking, business travel expenses, freelancer tax record organization, invoicing, estimates, payment receipts, customer records, budgeting, reporting, and AI-assisted financial questions.

## Problems & Pain Points

**Core problem:** Financial records are captured in disconnected tools or too late to be reliable, making it hard to prove expenses, track money owed, and understand the current position.

**Why alternatives fall short:**

- Paper receipts disappear and manual spreadsheets depend on perfect habits.
- Single-purpose tools break the relationship between a receipt, transaction, budget, invoice, payment, and report.
- Large accounting products can feel heavier than the immediate job requires.

**What it costs them:** Time spent re-entering data, missed context, slower follow-up, uncertain balances, and stressful reconciliation.

**Emotional tension:** Users want to feel in control and prepared, not guilty, confused, or surprised by their own records.

## Competitive Landscape

**Direct:** Receipt scanners, expense trackers, invoicing products, and small-business finance suites. FreshBooks and Wave are design and information-architecture references for this project, not copy or branding sources.

**Secondary:** Spreadsheets, note apps, cloud folders, and generic document generators.

**Indirect:** Delaying record keeping until a deadline or handing unstructured material to an accountant.

## Differentiation

**Key differentiators:**

- Connected product story: receipt scanning to expense tracking to budgeting to reporting.
- Connected sales story: estimate to invoice to payment to receipt to reporting.
- Web and mobile surfaces with a shared Receipt Cycle visual language.
- AI assistance grounded in the user's saved records, where supported by the current product.

**How we do it differently:** Receipt Cycle treats capture as the beginning of a record's useful life rather than the end of a scanning task.

**Why that's better:** Users can move from proof to action without reconstructing the same information across unrelated tools.

**Why customers choose us:** A focused workflow for real small-business finance jobs with less administrative friction.

| I do not want a complicated accounting system. | Lead with the specific workflow the visitor needs and show the real interface early. Do not position unsupported accounting functions. |

**Anti-persona:** Organizations seeking payroll, inventory management, automatic tax filing, or deep accounting and banking integrations that Receipt Cycle does not currently provide.

## Switching Dynamics

**Push:** Lost receipts, stale spreadsheets, scattered client records, unpaid invoices, and last-minute report preparation.

**Pull:** Fast capture, connected workflows, visible status, real product proof, and a clear next action.

**Habit:** Existing spreadsheets, folders, and delayed monthly or annual cleanup.

**Anxiety:** Migration effort, data accuracy, security, platform fit, and whether the product genuinely supports the advertised workflow.

## Brand Voice

**Tone:** Calm, practical, credible, and direct.

**Style:** Concrete product language, short sentences, specific outcomes, and no inflated promises. Visible landing-page copy must not use em dashes.

**Personality:** Clear, capable, grounded, helpful, and respectful.

## Commercial Landing Page Hero Rules

These rules come from direct review feedback and apply to every commercial landing page unless a page brief explicitly overrides them:

- Keep the hero inside a deliberate centered content width. Do not let its text and product visual feel stretched from edge to edge.
- Use a short, natural H1. Break lines according to meaning, not simply to fill space. A restrained brand-color emphasis may distinguish the audience or outcome.
- Do not place generic category eyebrows such as "Receipt Cycle invoicing" above the H1.
- Use one primary hero CTA. Do not add a secondary "see how it works" action unless it answers a documented visitor need.
- Keep reassurance copy below the CTA brief and verified. "No credit card required" and "Cancel paid plans anytime" are currently supported.
- Do not add a row of checkmarked feature fragments beneath the CTA by default.
- Limit the hero description to one concise sentence that explains the practical outcome. Save detailed features and keyword coverage for the sections below.
- Avoid labels that merely name the page, workflow, or product category without helping the visitor make a decision.

## Commercial Landing Page Originality Rules

- Reuse the Receipt Cycle design system, not another page's prose. Every commercial page must have original substantive headings, explanations, examples, comparisons, transitions, FAQ answers, and metadata written for its own search intent.
- Do not copy or lightly paraphrase published wording from competitors, templates, or any other online page. Research may shape the strategy, but the final language must be written specifically for Receipt Cycle.
- Do not manufacture a new page by swapping a keyword, audience, or feature name into sentences taken from an existing landing page.
- Shared navigation labels, footer labels, legal language, verified reassurance microcopy, stable product terminology, and the repeated primary CTA within one page are acceptable common elements.
- Before publishing a new page, compare it with all existing commercial landing pages and rewrite unexplained matching sentences, distinctive phrases, or paragraph structures.

## Landing Page Illustration and Responsive QA Rules

- Do not rely only on product screenshots. Use original editorial illustrations where a human situation or audience use case is easier to understand visually.
- Audience selectors should update the illustration to match the selected persona instead of leaving a generic text-only panel beside the selector.
- Keep audience sections consistent across commercial pages: a large landscape illustration panel paired with four accessible persona tabs or accordions. Each persona needs its own original scene and useful copy.
- Feature sections should follow the established visual mosaic pattern. Give each major capability its own product visual, document preview, status panel, timeline, or other relevant artwork instead of filling a uniform grid with text-only cards.
- When a workflow explanation contains several stacked steps, use a taller companion visual that spans the height of the explanation rather than placing a short square graphic beside it.
- Break longer card copy into short paragraphs. Avoid dense text blocks, repeated adjectives, and reusing the same qualifier across several headings.
- Competitor illustrations may guide medium, energy, and broad interaction patterns, but Receipt Cycle artwork must use original people, scenes, composition, palette, and copy. Never reuse competitor logos or artwork.
- Keep illustration assets landscape-oriented with safe margins so they crop cleanly inside rounded cards.
- Optimize production artwork before embedding it. Prefer a modern web format and declare intrinsic dimensions to prevent layout shift.
- After every landing-page change, verify the production build and inspect behavior at mobile, tablet, laptop, and wide-desktop widths. Check wrapping, cropping, tap targets, overflow, readability, and keyboard accessibility.
